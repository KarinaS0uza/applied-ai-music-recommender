# Music description guide

<!-- Read by src/knowledge.py: each "## " section below becomes one retrievable guide document. -->

Project-authored vocabulary and retrieval guidance. These sections explain requests;
they are not evidence that any particular recording has a trait.

## Pace, rhythm and energy

Tempo describes musical pace; perceived energy also depends on dynamics, texture,
rhythm and delivery. Slow does not necessarily mean calm or sad. Fast does not
necessarily mean happy. Running, workout and dance requests may favor a steady
pulse, but suitability is personal. Do not invent BPM or audio measurements.

## Instrumental piano and concentration

Instrumental means no sung or spoken vocals in the requested recording. Piano can
be solo, accompanied, acoustic or electronic. Calm piano for studying is a listener
preference, not a proven cognitive benefit. A piano credit alone does not prove
there are no vocals. Require explicit instrumental evidence for a no-vocals request.

## Vocal delivery and emotion

Breathy, clear, raspy, expressive, whispered, clean singing and screaming describe
different vocal qualities. Sad lyrics, a slow tempo or a ballad label do not establish
emotionally expressive delivery. A heavy guitar track can have clean or harsh
vocals. For no screaming, require evidence about delivery in the actual version;
missing vocal information is unknown. State subjective fit tentatively.

## Language, genre and cultural context

Keep unfamiliar genres and languages as requested. Portuguese / português is not
the same as Cape Verdean Creole. An artist's nationality does not establish a song's
language. Samba, bossa nova, raï, son cubano, K-pop, jazz, metal, ambient and
electronic dance music differ; do not collapse them into generic pop. Translations,
covers and remixes may change language and instrumentation.

## Instrumentation and texture

Distinguish piano, guitar, bass, drum, percussion, synthesizer, strings, saxophone and
trumpet when sources identify them. Acoustic is not synonymous with instrumental.
Sparse means few simultaneous layers, not necessarily low tempo. A genre name
does not prove a specific instrument. Portuguese percussion requests need actual
percussion evidence, not just an inference from samba or nationality.

## Reference songs, versions and exclusions

A reference song anchors a comparison; it does not replace the user's description.
Identify its artist and version when possible. Compare only documented shared
traits. Originals, live performances, remixes, radio edits and covers can differ.
Exclude a candidate when a hard requirement is contradicted or remains unknown.
Explain missing information and return fewer results when necessary. Recording
identity metadata does not prove mood, instrumental status or listening suitability.
